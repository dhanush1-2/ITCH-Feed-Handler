**Nothing is labelled unexplained, but three explanations stop at what this feed can show.** The
reason a reference number precedes its display, the order types behind the C prints, and why a
halted book may cross are all inferences about Nasdaq's engine. Each is marked "inference" below.

**A consequence to act on.** Both the C++ book and the Python reference queue orders by message
arrival, and they agree exactly. After the open that is not the exchange's priority at some
levels: see "Queue position" under Limits.

## Known-answer comparison

`verify/reference_book.py` was written from the spec's field tables before `include/itch` or
`include/book` were read. It uses one dict of orders and no price levels or queues; a book exists
only when a checkpoint sorts the live orders.

The first full-day run differed in one field only, `trade_count`, on 8 of 20 symbols. Root cause:
the C++ counted a `Q` with zero shares as a trade. Spec 1.5.2 says such a message means no cross
took place. 12,189 of the day's 17,836 `Q` messages have zero shares, so the day's trade count was
overstated by exactly that: 7,050,670 before, 7,038,481 after. Printed volume did not change. Fixed
in `include/book/stats.hpp` with a regression test (`Stats.ZeroShareCrossIsNotATrade`).

After the fix the comparison is exact for all 20 symbols: message counts by type, share totals,
printed volume, trade count, and levels and FIFO lists at both checkpoints. The noon checkpoint
compares 121,587 resting orders. The end-of-day checkpoint compares none, because the day ends
empty, and `compare.py` refuses to pass if every checkpoint is empty.

Separately, `verify/recon_book.py`, also independent of the C++ book, reproduced every oracle count
exactly: `exec_checked`, `exec_not_best`, `exec_not_head`, their continuous splits, touches, crossed
touches and episodes.

## exec_not_head: 92,941 of 5,822,741 executions

**Observed.** 92,902 in continuous trading. By message: 78,520 printable C, 10,565 E, 3,856
non-printable C. The split matters: 82,376 of the 99,644 C messages that hit a best level are not
at the head (83%), against 10,565 of 5,722,824 E messages (0.18%). These are different things.

### E messages: 10,565

**First instance.** PTI, t = 34200104851493 ns, 09:30:00.105. Order 5462951 executes at rank 1
behind order 6160383. Arrival order says 6160383 came first, at 09:24:18; order 5462951 appeared on
the feed only at 09:30:00.095, yet has the lower reference number. More in
`exec_not_head.first_E_instances`.

**Hypotheses tested.**

| Hypothesis | Result |
|---|---|
| Same-timestamp batches: the skipped order fills in the same event, just reported later | Insufficient. True for 6,565; the other 4,000 are not. |
| Self-match prevention: same MPID as the head | Rejected. 0 share an MPID; only 73 have one at all. |
| A same-price replace keeps its priority | Rejected. 0 of the executed orders came from a same-price `U`. |
| Odd-lot head | Insufficient. 7,687 have an odd-lot head; the rest do not. |
| Queue order follows the reference number, not message arrival | **Holds in every case.** |

**Evidence.** For every execution at a best level, both queue models were evaluated:

| | E messages |
|---|---|
| At the best level | 5,722,824 |
| Executed order is the lowest reference number at the level | 5,722,824 |
| Executed order is the first to arrive at the level | 5,712,259 |
| Models disagree, and reference order was right | 10,565 |
| Models disagree, and arrival order was right | **0** |

The inversions themselves are confined to the open. An inversion is an add arriving at a level with
a lower reference number than an order already there. There are 360,450 of them, and all fall
between 0.001 s and 39.7 s after 09:30:00, with none in the rest of the feed and none from a `U`. All 10,565 executed orders were displayed within 31.4 s of 09:30:00, though the executions
themselves run until 15:59, as those orders rest. Dating each reference number by the feed second
at which that number was first reached puts all 10,565 before the open, a median of 8,888 s before
their add appeared.

**Conclusion.** Explained: Project 1's belief holds once "head" means the lowest reference number.
Inference: spec 1.3.1 says the reference is assigned "at the time of receipt", and spec 1.1 says the
`Q` event means "Market Hours orders are available for execution", which fits orders received
before the open, held out of the display, and released after it with their original priority. The
feed shows the ordering and the timing, not the holding.

### C messages away from the display price: 82,356

**First instances.** `exec_not_head.C.away_from_display_price.first_instances`.

**Evidence.** Spec 1.4.2 defines C as an execution "at a price different from the initial display
price". On a buy the trade is above the display price, and on a sell below it, in 82,345 of 82,356;
the 11 others are all non-printable and outside continuous trading. Printable ones (78,520) land at
the midpoint of display and opposite best (38,741), at the opposite best (37,057) or between
(2,722), and never outside.

The order at the head of the display level is resting at the display price, so it could not have
filled at a price beyond its limit. Being skipped is not a queue violation.

**Conclusion.** Explained as not a queue event: the oracle compares such a C with a level the trade
did not happen at. The other 15,115 such messages were at the head, which says nothing about queue order
either. **The order types are not visible on
this feed.** Price-to-comply and midpoint behavior would fit the prices (inference); nothing here
shows which order type any individual message was.

### C messages at the display price: 20

**Evidence.** All 20 are non-printable and outside continuous trading. Every non-printable message
of the day is accounted for under "Match numbers": none of these 20 is in a pair, so all are cross
allocations, within 1.214 ms of a nonzero cross at the cross price. 11 are at the lowest reference;
9 are not.

**Conclusion.** Explained as cross allocation. Cross allocation order is not queue order and the
spec does not define it, so the 9 are not a counterexample to the reference-order finding, which is
stated for E messages.

## exec_not_best: 273

**Observed.** All 273 are non-printable C and none is in continuous trading. MKD has 218; ARDS,
QUIK, WKEY, MBOT, SLGL, CETXP, ZWZZT, ZJZZT and YI have the rest. 272 hit a book that was already
crossed.

**First instances.** ZJZZT at 36012444352514, ZWZZT at 36022633526282 and 36022633526540, CETXP at
36287932264010. More in `exec_not_best.first_instances`.

**Hypothesis.** These are the allocations of a halt cross, reported one order at a time in an order
that does not walk the book from best to worst.

**Evidence.** For all 273 the nearest cross on that symbol is type `H`, the execution price equals
that cross's price, and it is at most 1.124 ms away. Spec 1.4.2 says non-printable shares go into a
later bulk print.

**Conclusion.** Explained. Not a book error.

## crossed_other: 8,579 touches in 30 episodes

**Observed.** 0 crossed touches and 0 episodes in continuous trading. The 30 episodes account for
all 8,579 touches, on 13 symbols: MKD 8, WKEY 6, ARDS 5, CETXP 2, and PNRL, IPDN, SVA, DNJR, ZWZZT,
SLGL, QUIK, MBOT, YI 1 each. All are listed in `crossed.episodes_listed`.

**First instance.** PNRL crosses at t = 28850929245139 (bid 1.42, ask 1.30), in state `H`, on an add.

**Hypothesis.** While a symbol is halted or paused nothing matches, so an accepted add can sit
through the opposite side.

**Evidence.** All 30 start in state `H` (6) or `P` (24), and all 30 are started by an add (A 29, F 1).
20 end on a C, the cross's allocations, and 10 on a D. 4 end only outside market hours (PNRL, IPDN,
SVA, DNJR): symbols still halted, cleared by end-of-day deletes.

**Conclusion.** Explained. That the engine accepts without matching during a halt is an inference;
the correlation is 30 of 30.

## Match numbers on two messages: 4,324

**Observed.** The brief expected match numbers to be disjoint across E, C and P, and spec 1.4.1
calls them day-unique. The data disagrees: 4,324 of 7,054,855 match numbers appear on two messages.

**First instance.** SVRA, match 50460, t = 34086523596953: C on order 9489356 marked non-printable,
then E on order 9489320, adjacent in the file. More in
`match_numbers.first_instances`.

**Evidence.**

| Pair (first, then second) | Count | Shares |
|---|---|---|
| Non-printable C + printable E | 4,250 | 557,515 |
| Non-printable C + printable P | 68 | 8,760 |
| Non-printable C + printable C | 6 | 1,320 |

Across all 4,324: same timestamp, same shares, same symbol, and exactly one printable message. In
the 4,256 where both orders are known, they are on opposite sides. No pair involves a Q.

**Conclusion.** Explained: two sides of one match between orders on the book, with the second side
marked non-printable. **Printed volume does not double count**, because the existing rule already
drops non-printable C. No volume change was needed, and 971,016,019 shares stands. Summing every
execution without the printable filter would double count 567,595 shares, which is the error spec
1.4.2 warns about.

The other 4,185 non-printable messages are cross allocations: each is at most 1.214 ms from a
nonzero cross on its symbol, at the cross price (C 2,607, O 860, H 718). That accounts for all 8,509.

**Cross ledger.** Same-timestamp non-printable shares total 2,939,517 against 114,191,631 Q shares
and never exceed a cross's Q shares; they equal it in 112 of 5,647 nonzero crosses. So Q is not
reconstructible from displayed fills, consistent with hidden and on-close interest (inference). No
printable execution falls in the 1 ms before a nonzero cross. The window after a cross was not
measured, so that a printable E never belongs to a cross is not proven.

## Halt-grace sensitivity

**Observed.** Crossed touches counted as continuous: 634 at 0 s of grace, 0 at 5 s, 0 at 30 s.

**Evidence.** All 634 are on a symbol that has resumed and whose cross has not arrived, at most
1.214 ms after the resume. They are the cross being reported. Executions classified as continuous
fall as grace grows (5,779,344, 5,778,220, 5,777,110), so longer grace discards real trading.

**Conclusion.** Explained. 5 s has about 4,000 times the margin the data needs, and 30 s buys nothing
for its cost. Kept at 5 s. The classification rule is measured on this day only.

## Limits

- **Queue position.** The C++ book and the reference both append by arrival, so their FIFO lists
  agree with each other and not always with the exchange's order. At levels holding an inversion,
  from the open until those orders leave, arrival-order rank is wrong. Price levels, share totals
  and the ledger are unaffected. Inserting by reference number within a level would bring
  `exec_not_head` for E to 0 on this day; that has not been built or tested in the handler.
- **One day.** Everything here is measured on 2019-12-30. The pins apply to this file and the slice.
- **No broken trades.** The day has no `B`, so that path is covered by synthetic tests only.
